Circuit

The topology model.mjs simulates, live at your settings: six ideal switches with antiparallel diodes, a stiff DC link, and a star load with a floating neutral. A lit switch is gated on, a lit diode carries current, and the numbers are the model's own pole voltages and phase currents. The phase wires leave the midpoints to the right of the bridge, and where they cross the N rail the crossing is drawn as a hop.

Symbols and conventions

One convention runs through everything below, and it is the one the model uses: m is normalised so that the ideal phase-voltage fundamental is m·Vdc/2. Half the literature writes m = 2/√3 at the hexagon limit; here the limit is m = 2/√3 ≈ 1.1547 and the panel flags anything above m = 1 as overmodulation.

One leg is a switch

Each leg connects its phase to either the P or the N rail. With sa ∈ {0,1} the upper device state, the pole voltage about the DC midpoint is a two-valued function, and the gates are complementary so the leg is never open — the ideal model states this assumption rather than hiding it.

vaN=(sa-12)Vdcv_{aN} = \left(s_a - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) V_{dc}(1)

s_a = 1 puts the pole at +Vdc/2, s_a = 0 at −Vdc/2.

salower=1-sa,tdead=0s_{a\,lower} = 1 - s_a, \qquad t_{dead} = 0(2)

Complementary gates; the dead time is zero in this model, shown as 0.00 µs on the panel.

Reference and regular sampling

The modulator compares a three-phase reference with a triangular carrier at fsw = 4 kHz. The model uses regular sampling: the reference is read once at the start of each carrier period and held, so the duty is exact for that period and there is no sample-grid quantisation error.

va*(t)=mVdc2sinθ,θ=ωt=2πftv_a^{*}(t) = \frac{m V_{dc}}{2}\sin\theta, \qquad \theta = \omega t = 2\pi f t(3)

The reference the panel draws thin and dashed.

da(k)=12(1+msinθk),θk=ωtkd_a(k) = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + m\sin\theta_k\right), \qquad \theta_k = \omega t_k(4)

Solving carrier = reference exactly gives the on-fraction held for carrier period k.

⟨vaN⟩=da(k)Vdc-Vdc2=mVdc2sinθk\langle v_{aN}\rangle = d_a(k)\, V_{dc} - \frac{V_{dc}}{2} = \frac{m V_{dc}}{2}\sin\theta_k(5)

Averaging the two-valued pole voltage over one carrier removes the switching, not the fundamental.

The load, and the neutral point

The load is star-connected, R = 2 Ω + L = 4 mH per phase, with no neutral wire. The star point therefore floats, and its voltage is whatever makes the three currents sum to zero. That is why the line-to-line voltages are the clean ones: the neutral term cancels.

vaN-vnN=Ria+Ldiadtv_{aN} - v_{nN} = R i_a + L\frac{d i_a}{dt}(6)

Kirchhoff per phase, with v_nN the star-point voltage about the DC midpoint.

vnN=13(vaN+vbN+vcN)v_{nN} = \frac{1}{3}\left(v_{aN} + v_{bN} + v_{cN}\right)(7)

Adding the three phase equations with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0 leaves this identity.

vab=vaN-vbN=R(ia-ib)+Ld(ia-ib)dtv_{ab} = v_{aN} - v_{bN} = R(i_a - i_b) + L\frac{d(i_a - i_b)}{dt}(8)

The neutral term is absent, so v_ab is a direct function of the switching state.

Z=R+jωL,ϕ=arctanωLR\mathbf{Z} = R + j\omega L, \qquad \varphi = \arctan\frac{\omega L}{R}(9)

At f = 50 Hz, ωL = 1.257 Ω against R = 2 Ω, so φ = 32.1° and pf = cos φ = 0.847.

Space vectors and the linear limit

The six switch states that connect every phase to a rail give six active vectors plus two zero vectors. Their tips form a hexagon, and the largest circle inside it is the largest balanced sine the inverter can produce without distortion.

v→=23(vaN+avbN+a2vcN),a=ej2π/3\vec{v} = \frac{2}{3}\left(v_{aN} + a\,v_{bN} + a^2 v_{cN}\right), \qquad a = e^{j 2\pi/3}(10)

The amplitude-invariant Clarke transform the Space vector plot draws.

v→k=23Vdcej(k-1)π/3,k=1…6\vec{v}_k = \frac{2}{3}V_{dc}\, e^{j(k-1)\pi/3}, \qquad k = 1\ldots 6(11)

Six vectors on the hexagon, radius 2/3·Vdc.

|v→|max=23Vdccos30∘=Vdc3|\vec{v}|_{max} = \frac{2}{3}V_{dc}\cos 30^{\circ} = \frac{V_{dc}}{\sqrt{3}}(12)

The inscribed circle: the radius where the reference stops touching the hexagon sides.

mlimVdc2=Vdc3⇒mlim=23≈1.1547\frac{m_{lim} V_{dc}}{2} = \frac{V_{dc}}{\sqrt{3}} \;\Rightarrow\; m_{lim} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 1.1547(13)

Beyond this the legs saturate; the panel's sweep shows V_LL falling below the dashed ideal there.

VLL=322mVdc≈0.6124mVdcV_{LL} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{2}}\, m\, V_{dc} \approx 0.6124\, m\, V_{dc}(14)

Line-to-line rms of the fundamental: √3 × (m Vdc/2)/√2. At m = 0.8, Vdc = 400 V this is 196.0 V.

What the spectrum contains

Regularly sampled PWM puts energy at the carrier and its sidebands, not at harmonics of the output. The fundamental is exact; everything else the panel's Spectrum plot shows is either a sideband or a genuine distortion product, and the THD it reports says which.

f=kfsw±nf,k=1,2,…,n=0,1,2,…f = k f_{sw} \pm n f, \qquad k = 1, 2, \ldots,\; n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots(15)

The sidebands sit around multiples of the carrier, spaced by the output frequency.

v^k,n∝Jn(nπm2)\hat{v}_{k,n} \propto J_n\!\left(\frac{n\pi m}{2}\right)(16)

Bessel weights: the sideband amplitude is set by m, which is why the carrier stays put as m changes.

THD=∑h≥2Vh2V1,hf<0.45fsw\mathrm{THD} = \frac{\sqrt{\sum_{h \ge 2} V_h^2}}{V_1}, \qquad h f < 0.45 f_{sw}(17)

Orders above 0.45·fsw are counted as modulation, not distortion; that cutoff is stated on the plot.

Vh=2T|∫0Tva(t)e-jhωtdt|,T=1fV_h = \frac{2}{T}\left|\int_{0}^{T} v_a(t)\, e^{-j h \omega t}\, dt\right|, \qquad T = \frac{1}{f}(18)

A rectangular window over whole fundamental periods, so neighbouring orders are exactly orthogonal.

Six-step, for comparison

Holding each active vector for a sixth of a cycle gives the largest fundamental a voltage-source inverter can make, at the cost of a staircase waveform and heavy low-order harmonics. The STEP mode and the vector switch drive exactly this.

v^1=2πVdc,VLL,1=32·2πVdc≈0.780Vdc\hat{v}_{1} = \frac{2}{\pi}V_{dc}, \qquad V_{LL,1} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}V_{dc} \approx 0.780\, V_{dc}(19)

The classic six-step fundamental, about 10 % above the linear PWM limit at the same Vdc.

THD6=π29-1≈0.311\mathrm{THD}_{6} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi^2}{9} - 1} \approx 0.311(20)

Only 6k ± 1 orders survive; the square-wave figure √(π²/8 − 1) = 48.3 % is the single-phase case and does not apply here.

Currents, power and ripple

The panel's meters are these four lines evaluated at your settings, plus the peak-to-peak ripple that the window averages deliberately hide.

Irms=Vrms|Z|=mVdc/(22)R2+(ωL)2I_{rms} = \frac{V_{rms}}{|\mathbf{Z}|} = \frac{m V_{dc}/(2\sqrt{2})}{\sqrt{R^2 + (\omega L)^2}}(21)

At the defaults this gives 47.8 A against the panel's 47.8 A.

P=3Irms2R,Q=3Irms2ωL,S=3VrmsIrmsP = 3 I_{rms}^2 R, \qquad Q = 3 I_{rms}^2 \omega L, \qquad S = 3 V_{rms} I_{rms}(22)

13.73 kW, 8.64 kvar, 16.22 kVA at the defaults; P and Q add in quadrature to S.

pf=PS=cosϕ\mathrm{pf} = \frac{P}{S} = \cos\varphi(23)

0.847 here, and identical for a linear load in a balanced three-phase system.

Δipp≈VdcLfswd(1-d)\Delta i_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{dc}}{L f_{sw}}\, d(1-d)(24)

Worst case at d = 0.5 is 400/(0.004·4000) = 25 A peak-to-peak; at the defaults the meters report 5.47 A because each phase sits nearer d = 0.2–0.8 than 0.5.

The rotating frame

Park-transforming into a frame that turns with the fundamental makes the steady-state vector DC. The cross-coupling terms are the price: the d and q axes see each other's current.

vd=Rid+Ldiddt-ωLiqvq=Riq+Ldiqdt+ωLid\begin{aligned} v_d &= R i_d + L\frac{d i_d}{dt} - \omega L i_q \\ v_q &= R i_q + L\frac{d i_q}{dt} + \omega L i_d \end{aligned}(25)

The same phase equation, rotated; ωL i_q and ωL i_d are the cross-coupling.

vd=23(vacosθ+vbcos(θ-2π3)+vccos(θ+2π3))vq=-23(vasinθ+vbsin(θ-2π3)+vcsin(θ+2π3))\begin{aligned} v_d &= \tfrac{2}{3}\left(v_a\cos\theta + v_b\cos(\theta - \tfrac{2\pi}{3}) + v_c\cos(\theta + \tfrac{2\pi}{3})\right) \\ v_q &= -\tfrac{2}{3}\left(v_a\sin\theta + v_b\sin(\theta - \tfrac{2\pi}{3}) + v_c\sin(\theta + \tfrac{2\pi}{3})\right) \end{aligned}(26)

Amplitude-invariant Park transform, aligned to the fundamental the panel tracks.

Devices and the DC link

Every number in the Devices plot comes from these two identities over the model's own states, not from a lookup table.

idc=saia+sbib+scici_{dc} = s_a i_a + s_b i_b + s_c i_c(27)

The DC-link current is the sum of the phase currents the upper devices are carrying; it pulses at 6f.

DS=1T∫0Tsdt,DD=1T∫0Ts¯|i|>0dtD_{S} = \frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T} s\, dt, \qquad D_{D} = \frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T} \bar{s}\,|i| > 0\, dt(28)

Conduction share per device over one fundamental period; the diode share is non-zero even at high pf.

IS,rms=IrmsDSI_{S,rms} = I_{rms}\sqrt{D_{S}}(29)

The RMS current a device has to carry, which is what sizing actually needs.

Worked example at the defaults

m = 0.80, Vdc = 400 V, f = 50 Hz, R = 2 Ω, L = 4 mH. Each line is the equation above followed by the panel's own number.

VLL=0.6124×0.80×400=196.0V(computed 195.8V)V_{LL} = 0.6124 \times 0.80 \times 400 = 196.0\ \mathrm{V} \;\; (\text{computed } 195.8\ \mathrm{V})(30)

The 0.1 % gap is the switching and the finite integration step, not a modelling error.

Vph=196.03=113.1V(panel 113.0V)V_{ph} = \frac{196.0}{\sqrt{3}} = 113.1\ \mathrm{V} \;\; (\text{panel } 113.0\ \mathrm{V})(31)

Phase rms, the first meter cell.

Irms=113.022+1.25662=47.8AI_{rms} = \frac{113.0}{\sqrt{2^2 + 1.2566^2}} = 47.8\ \mathrm{A}(32)

Exactly the panel's current meter.

P=3×47.82×2=13.71kW(panel 13.73kW)P = 3 \times 47.8^2 \times 2 = 13.71\ \mathrm{kW} \;\; (\text{panel } 13.73\ \mathrm{kW})(33)

The two-hundredths agree to rounding in I_rms.

What this model does not contain

These are the equations of an ideal inverter. The page states the gaps rather than pretending they are small, because an engineer sizing hardware needs to know which way each one moves the answer.

No dead time (0.00 µs shown), no switch on-state drop, no switching loss, no reverse recovery, a stiff DC link with no ripple, a linear L with no saturation, no iron or copper loss, no thermal model, and illustrative component dimensions. Computed values, not measurements — nothing here has been validated against hardware.
